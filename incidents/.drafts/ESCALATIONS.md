## 2021-10-24_badrabbit — likely misdated (DZSF 2021-10-24 has no open-source corroboration; BadRabbit is a one-shot 2017-10-24 event) [RESOLVED 2026-04-19]
- Searches attempted: 20 distinct queries across 5 rounds (English, German, Russian, Ukrainian) plus 9 WebFetches
- Independent non-DZSF sources found: 14 (all describing the 2017 BadRabbit campaign; **none** documenting a 2021 event)
- Specific gap or conflict: The DZSF raw-data row places a BadRabbit ransomware railway incident in Ukraine on **2021-10-24**. No open-source reporting — vendor analyses (Kaspersky Securelist, ESET WeLiveSecurity, Cisco Talos, Trend Micro, Check Point, Fortinet), government attribution (UK NCSC 3 Oct 2018), threat-intel trackers (MITRE ATT&CK S0606, CFR Cyber Operations Tracker), or press (Newsweek, TechCrunch, SecurityWeek, The Hacker News) — documents any BadRabbit activity in October 2021. Every credible source dates the single BadRabbit / Win32/Diskcoder.D outbreak to **2017-10-24**, exactly four years earlier to the day, and lists the Kyiv Metro fare-payment network and Odessa International Airport among its Ukrainian transport victims. The 2021 date is almost certainly a clerical year typo (2017 → 2021), possibly introduced when the DZSF raw-data spreadsheet was re-exported in 2021/2022. The subagent did not silently rewrite the date per protocol.
- Subagent recommendation: **annotate → correct** — change `event_date` and filename stem to `2017-10-24_badrabbit`, then promote with the full 2017 BadRabbit narrative (Kyiv Metro, Odessa airport, Ukrainian Ministry of Infrastructure; MITRE S0606 technique list; Sandworm / GRU Unit 74455 attribution per UK NCSC Oct 2018).
- Maintainer decision (2026-04-19): **correct date to 2017-10-24 and promote.** Renamed the draft files from `2021-10-24_badrabbit.{yaml,log.md}` to `2017-10-24_badrabbit.{yaml,log.md}`. Updated the `id` and `event_date` fields inside the YAML accordingly. Adopted the full 2017 BadRabbit narrative (Kyiv Metro anchor + Odesa Airport + Ukrainian Ministry of Infrastructure; Sandworm / GRU Unit 74455 attribution per UK NCSC 3 Oct 2018; MITRE techniques T1486, T1490, T1210, T1078, T1608.001; AES-128 / RSA-2048 / EternalRomance detail) into the draft's typed fields and `description_en` / `description_de`. `needs_review` flipped to `false` on both the top-level and `provenance` levels (≥ 6 independent non-DZSF sources — Kaspersky, ESET, Check Point, CFR, SecurityWeek / UK-NCSC attribution, MITRE — comfortably clear the ≥ 2 threshold).
